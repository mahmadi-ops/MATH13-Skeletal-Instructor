Example 4.8.7. Absolute Extrema on a Circle.

Find the absolute maximum and minimum of \(f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 2x + 2y + 5\text{,}\) given the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\text{.}\)
🔗
Completing the squares shows that
\begin{equation} f(x,y) = (x-1)^2 + (y+1)^2 + 3,\tag{4.8.8} \end{equation}
so the graph of \(f\) is a paraboloid with vertex at \((1,-1,3)\text{,}\) and the level curves of \(f\) are the circles \((x-1)^2 + (y+1)^2 = c - 3\) centered at \((1,-1)\text{.}\) The constraint \(g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4 = 0\) is a circle of radius \(2\) centered at the origin. Restricting \(f\) to that circle traces a curve on the paraboloid, and we are after the highest and lowest points of that curve.
