Example 4.8.5. Extrema of \(f(x,y) = xy\) on an Ellipse.

Find the greatest and smallest values that the function \(f(x,y) = xy\) takes over the ellipse
\begin{equation} \frac{x^2}{8} + \frac{y^2}{2} = 1.\tag{4.8.7} \end{equation}
🔗
Solution. (First rewrite (4.8.7) in the form \(g = 0\) that (4.8.6) requires, then compute both gradients and read the vector equation one component at a time. Substituting one component equation into the other clears \(x\) and leaves an equation in \(\lambda\) alone; it has a second root \(y = 0\text{,}\) which must be discarded — say why. Finish with the constraint, evaluate \(f\) at the points you get, and say what makes the largest and smallest of those values the absolute extrema.)
